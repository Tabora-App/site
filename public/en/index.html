An ad-free browser with mail and a wallet

Tabora removes ads from pages, remembers your passwords and fills in sign-in codes for you. It has mail with a separate address for every site and a crypto wallet. There’s nothing extra to install.

Download for macOS Source code on GitHub

Free. Version 0.3.0 for Mac with Apple silicon (M1 and later).

Pages open without ads

Tabora doesn’t load ad banners or the trackers that follow you from site to site. Pages open faster, and you see only what you came for.

Works right after you install it
There’s nothing to turn on or set up. The browser updates its lists of ad addresses on its own.
You can see what was blocked
A counter in the toolbar shows how many requests were blocked on the page. Click it to see the list of addresses.
If a site breaks
Sometimes a site doesn’t work properly without its ads. You can turn blocking off for just that site in one click. It stays on everywhere else.
Ads in YouTube videos
When an ad starts in a video, the browser runs through it in a couple of seconds, without sound, and doesn’t show it. No need to press “Skip”.
Cookie pop-ups close on their own
The browser closes the “We use cookies” banner for you and chooses “Reject”.

The browser remembers your passwords and sign-in codes

Your site passwords and one-time codes (the ones an app on your phone usually shows) are kept in the browser, protected by a single password of yours. On a site, you just click the suggestion.

The code appears right under the field
When a site asks for a six-digit code, the browser offers it. No need to reach for your phone.
Codes move over from your phone
Codes from Google Authenticator, Aegis and 2FAS come over with a QR code. Passwords are imported as a file from another browser.
Passkeys instead of passwords
On sites that support passkeys you need no password: just confirm that it is you. Passkeys are kept next to your passwords.
A check for leaked passwords
The browser compares your passwords with a database of stolen ones and shows which to change. The passwords themselves never leave your computer.
Only you know your password
It’s never sent to our servers, so even we can’t open your passwords and codes.
If you forget your password
When you create an account, you get a recovery key. Keep it somewhere safe: it will get you back in.

Mail with a separate address for every site

Along with the browser you get a mailbox like name@tabora.app. When a site asks for your email, the browser offers a separate address just for that site. Messages arrive in your mailbox, and the site never learns your real address.

Tired of newsletters? Turn the address off
A site started sending you ads or passed your email on to someone else. Turn off the address you gave that site, and the messages stop coming.
Read and write mail in the browser
You don’t need a separate mail app. You can also add the mailboxes you already have.
You can encrypt your messages
A message to another Tabora address can be encrypted, so only the recipient can read it. The subject line stays unencrypted.
A notification tells you about new mail
No need to check your mailbox by hand.

The same passwords and bookmarks on your second computer

Sign in to your account on another computer, and your passwords, sign-in codes, bookmarks, history and the list of open tabs appear there.

Your data is encrypted before it’s sent
The server holds a copy that can’t be read without your password. We can’t read it either.
You choose what to sync
Passwords, passkeys, codes, bookmarks, tabs, history, addresses and settings are switched on one by one. Bank cards are synced only if you turn that on yourself. You can switch sync off and erase the copy from the server.
Send a page to another device
Found an article on one computer? Send it to another, and it opens there by itself.
The wallet stays on your computer
The wallet’s secret phrase is never sent anywhere.

A crypto wallet right in the browser

The wallet works with Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Polygon, Bitcoin, Solana and TRON. The secret phrase is encrypted and kept only on your computer.

Sites connect in one click
On exchanges and in apps like Uniswap, just click “Connect”. You don’t need a separate extension.
You can see what you’re signing
Before you confirm, the wallet shows in plain words how much will leave, who gets it and what permissions the site will get. Risky requests are flagged.
One coin, one line
USDT on different networks is shown as one total. You pick the network when you send or receive.
Protection from look-alike addresses
Scammers send tiny amounts from an address that looks like yours, hoping you’ll copy it later by mistake. The wallet hides these transfers from your history.

For developers: EIP-6963 and EIP-1193, Wallet Standard for Solana, TronLink compatibility. .eth names and ipfs:// links open from the address bar.

The agent fills in forms and finds messages for you

Tell the agent what you need: sign up on a site, find the message with a code, fill in a form. It does it in a browser tab while you watch.

It asks before sending
Before submitting a form, the agent shows what will be sent and to which site. Nothing is sent without your OK.
A page can’t give it orders
Text on a site or in a message can’t make the agent do something behind your back: once it has read a page, every action it takes needs your confirmation.

The agent needs your OpenAI key. The key is stored in the browser, protected by your password.

More in Tabora

Spaces
Personal, Work, Projects. Each has its own tabs, pinned sites and sign-ins: on the same site you can be in your work account in one space and your personal one in another.
Tabs on the side or on top
The tab list sits on the left and collapses to icons. If you’re used to tabs on top, turn them on in settings.
One bar for search ⌘K
It searches your open tabs and history, opens parts of the browser and takes tasks for the agent.
Sleeping tabs
Tabs you haven’t returned to in a while can be put to sleep so they don’t take up memory.
Tab groups and two sites side by side
Tabs gather into groups with a name and a color. Two sites can be shown side by side in one window.
Moving from another browser
Bookmarks, history and passwords come over from Chrome, Safari, Edge and other browsers in a couple of clicks.
Account protection
You can protect sign-in with a second step (a code from an app) and add a backup email in case you forget your password.
Support in the browser and on the web
Send a question or a bug report from the browser's settings or from support.tabora.app, with a screenshot if you like. On the site you can write even without an account.

The code is open, so you can check it

All of Tabora’s code is published on GitHub. You’ll also find the security review reports there, and a description of how passwords, mail and the wallet are protected.

Updates are signed
The browser installs only updates signed with Tabora’s key. The Mac installer is checked by Apple.
Built on the Firefox engine
Pages are displayed by the Firefox engine. Security fixes arrive with its releases.
Encryption libraries are verified
The code that encrypts passwords and signs transfers is built from open-source code at pinned versions. You can verify this with one command from the repository.
